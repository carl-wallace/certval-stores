# certval_stores_webpki_root_certs

The Mozilla root program's TLS server roots, as a `certval` trust store.

The material comes from the [`webpki-root-certs`](https://crates.io/crates/webpki-root-certs)
crate and is **not vendored here**: this crate holds no certificates of its own,
so the roots move when that dependency moves.

**Choose this crate over `certval_stores_mozilla` for the update cost, not for
the contents.** The two ship the same anchors -- measured 2026-09-21, this
crate's 121 certificates are byte-for-byte `certval_stores_mozilla::TLS_ROOTS`,
identical sets with nothing on either side. What differs is what it takes to
move them forward:

| | refreshing the roots | diff |
|---|---|---|
| `certval_stores_mozilla` | run `tools/refresh.py` and `tools/refresh_cas.py` against CCADB, regenerate `generated.rs`, rewrite `roots/` and `cas/` | ~14 MB of certificate bytes |
| this crate | `cargo update -p webpki-root-certs` | one line of `Cargo.lock` |

Two things that cuts both ways. Freshness becomes the rustls project's
discipline rather than yours: nobody here runs anything, and equally nobody here
decides when the trust material moves or reads a CCADB diff before it does. And
the saving covers the **anchors only** -- the trust bits,
`distrust_for_tls_after`, `mozilla_applied_constraints` and the 6.2 MB of CCADB
intermediates exist because something scraped for them, and no `cargo update`
produces them. This is not the cheap version of `certval_stores_mozilla`; it is
the cheap version of its anchor slice.

There is deliberately **no count or digest** pinning the material here, unlike
in the sibling crates. Theirs guard vendored bytes, where a moving count means a
local edit or a botched regeneration. Nothing here is local, so a pin would fail
on every legitimate `cargo update`, report only that something moved, and teach
whoever hits it to bump the constant unread. Reviewing each change to the trust
set is what `certval_stores_mozilla` is for; choosing this crate is choosing to
delegate that curation to the rustls and CCADB process. The signal that the
material moved is the `Cargo.lock` diff.

## Why `webpki-root-certs` and not `webpki-roots`

Both are generated by the rustls project from the same CCADB report, and
`webpki-roots` is the smaller of the two. It is the wrong one here.

`webpki-roots` ships a `TrustAnchor` of subject, public key and optional name
constraints, each with its outer SEQUENCE tag and length removed, and no key
identifier. That shape suits rustls, which is always handed a chain by the peer
and only has to match its top to an anchor. certval *builds* paths: it looks up
the issuer of a certificate by the authority key identifier that certificate
carries, so an anchor needs the identifier its subordinates actually reference.
Consuming `webpki-roots` means synthesizing one by hashing the public key, which
matches what the CA published only when the CA used RFC 5280 method (1).

`webpki-root-certs` ships whole certificates, so each anchor carries the CA's own
subject key identifier extension. It is also the `certificate` alternative of an
RFC 5914 `TrustAnchorChoice`, which is what `StoreEntry::roots` takes.

## Relationship to `certval_stores_mozilla`

They overlap, and neither replaces the other.

`certval_stores_mozilla` vendors its material, covers TLS **and** S/MIME roots
(121 and 91, 170 in its combined set), ships CCADB intermediates as a CA store,
and carries the trust bits and `distrust_for_tls_after` metadata that no webpki
crate has. Reach for it when any of that matters, or when you want to control
when the material moves.

This crate is anchors-only and TLS servers only, and owns no refresh procedure.
Reach for it when a consumer wants the public web PKI roots, nothing else, and
no maintenance.

A consumer that already depends on `certval_stores_mozilla` gains nothing here:
the anchors are the same bytes, and the reason to prefer this crate is a cost
that consumer is already paying.

Their store ids differ (`webpki_root_certs` here, `webpki` / `webpki_tls` /
`webpki_email` there), so both can be loaded together. A consumer that does so
should expect the same certificate to appear under two store names.

## Features

`reqwest-client` is the only one, forwarded to `certval_stores_core`. The store
itself is unconditional: this crate has one environment and the
`webpki-root-certs` dependency is the whole of it, so there is no configuration
in which the crate is present and provides nothing.

## Provenance

`published` and `collected` are both `None`, because the source states neither.
`webpki-root-certs` exposes a single constant and no report date, and this crate
collects nothing itself. The staleness bound available to a consumer is the
dependency version recorded in `Cargo.lock`.
